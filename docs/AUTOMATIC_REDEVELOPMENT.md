# Automatic redevelopment screen

The property endpoint populates a preliminary for-sale redevelopment screen for Cambridge C-1 parcels where the existing geometric rule engine can produce an envelope. Other zoning coverage does not imply a usable development envelope. The default report requires no inputs. An optional manual condo tool is available on reports without an automatic estimate.

## Program and revenue

Compare the base four-story envelope and, where the parcel passes the existing lot-area screen, the conditional six-story inclusionary envelope. Each uses the upper geometric area sensitivity. Screen out replacements that shrink reported gross or living area or reduce reported units. Rank by highest modeled surplus when all scenarios have enough evidence, otherwise show the largest remaining program. The report flags projects that lose money even in the upside case. The downside case stresses costs and prices for the same program. Footprint sensitivity is calculated separately.

Allow 18% of modeled gross area for common/service space. Fit whole units using assumed interior areas of 700, 1,050, and 1,400 sq ft and a target mix of 20% one-bedroom, 50% two-bedroom, and 30% three-bedroom, as in Table 34 of the [Cambridge September 2026 study](https://www.cambridgema.gov/-/media/Files/CDD/Housing/Inclusionary/cambridgeinclusionaryhousingstudy2026.pdf). Deduct unallocated area separately. Where the modeled project has at least ten units, gross envelope reaches 10,000 sq ft, or the option is conditional on inclusionary housing, reserve whole units covering at least 20% of the modeled unit area. Gross area is a conservative screening proxy for the ordinance's residential-space trigger. Confirm the actual statutory area calculation, exemptions, bedroom distribution and project approval under the [Cambridge inclusionary program](https://www.cambridgema.gov/CDD/housing/fordevelopersandpropmanagers/inclusionarydevelopers). Restricted-unit revenue is excluded until project-specific price limits are available.

The Cambridge screen uses the [city’s FY2026 assessor database](https://data.cambridgema.gov/d/waa7-ibdu). The search is citywide because unit coordinates are often missing. The assessor database reports a unit's latest sale at the time of the release, so it may omit subsequent sales and earlier transactions. Require a residential condominium, positive living area, sale within three years, recorded book/page, and closing in the reported year built or the following year. Reject possible bundled sales and count at most one sale per shared parcel identifier in each bedroom group as a project proxy. Assessor fields do not independently verify first sale, restricted pricing, arm’s-length status, condition or concessions. For each size, use the middle half of rates when at least three registry-confirmed project proxies fall within 15% of modeled living area and match bedroom and bathroom counts. If any market-rate unit type lacks three matches, leave the sellout and budget unpriced. An incomplete query does not set the rate.

## Costs

Study Table 27 supplies the $320/sq ft wood-frame and $372/sq ft podium hard-cost benchmarks. The downside case adds 10%. Ordinary demolition/abatement, sitework, civil/offsite and utility work, escalation and contingency are separately budgeted assumptions. Design, engineering, permitting, due diligence, legal, accounting, development management, insurance, security and temporary utilities are allowances derived from the project's modeled hard costs. The screen budgets for taxes, sales-period carry and initial condo reserves by unit. It models acquisition at the midpoint of the supported as-is market estimate, plus buyer closing costs. Without an as-is estimate, the budget excludes acquisition and surplus is withheld. It includes brokerage, marketing, closing, deeds excise and concessions as a combined sale-cost allowance rather than a statutory calculation.

Financing uses the study's 7.5% construction rate and 30% equity benchmark (Table 30) with assumed fees, construction and sales periods, and draw balances. The [OCC construction lending handbook](https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/commercial-real-estate-lending/index-commercial-real-estate-lending.html) informs the interest reserve approach. There is no permanent loan in this for-sale case. No dedicated parking construction or subsidy is assumed. Verify site-dependent costs such as extraordinary remediation, tenant relocation, unusual foundations, offsite work and parking. These can materially change the result. Developer income tax, equity distributions and preferred returns are outside the modeled project surplus.


## Current value and project value

The main report shows the assessor-reported owner or entity and links to the assessor source when available. A reviewed deed records a particular transfer. Current title requires a fresh deed check.

The as-is market screen uses at least three distinct MassLandRecords-confirmed sales of the same assessor class that have the city's qualifying sale flag. It matches living area within 15 percent and bedrooms and bathrooms within one. It starts within 0.5 mile and widens within the municipality only to find a third qualifying sale. The range scales the selected sale prices per living square foot to the subject and rounds outward to $10,000. It does not adjust for condition, timing, or other appraisal factors. The tax assessment appears separately with its fiscal year when reported. An insufficient sample leaves the market estimate unavailable.

Potential value at completion shows gross market-rate condo sales for the selected conceptual buildout, before project costs. PlotDocket leaves automatic redevelopment unpriced for individual condo accounts and lots with multiple assessor accounts. The buildout shows total modeled gross floor area and unit count, including restricted units. Cambridge's inclusionary threshold is at least 10 units or 10,000 gross residential square feet. A six-story option also requires at least 5,000 square feet of lot area and inclusionary compliance. See the [Cambridge plan form](https://www.cambridgema.gov/-/media/Files/CDD/Housing/Inclusionary/ihpplanform.pdf) and [multifamily ordinance amendments](https://www.cambridgema.gov/cdd/projects/zoning/multifamilyhousing).


## Cambridge parcel and approval screen

Use the selected parcel's exact map and lot to look up geometry in the [Cambridge parcel GIS](https://www.cambridgema.gov/gis/gisdatadictionary/assessing/fy2027/assessing_parcelsfy2027). If the city source cannot identify that parcel uniquely, the app can use its matched MassGIS polygon and labels the source. Assessed lot area stays separate from GIS geometry.

For the two reviewed Magazine Street parcels, the app shows the frontage annotated as a deed dimension on the FY2027 assessor block map when the map-lot and assessor address both match. The map shows 70.0 feet along Magazine Street for 95 Magazine St, map-lot 101-200, and 19.07 feet for 106 Magazine St, map-lot 102-51 within the 100–108 Magazine St row. The assessor maps are for assessment purposes. Use the recorded survey and site plan to confirm street lines, parcel geometry, and any eligible party-wall or setback exception before modeling a site-specific footprint. [Block 101](https://gis.cambridgema.gov/blockmaps/Block_101.pdf) · [Block 102](https://gis.cambridgema.gov/blockmaps/Block_102.pdf)

Screen the parcel against the city's [zoning overlays](https://www.cambridgema.gov/gis/gisdatadictionary/cdd/cdd_zoningoverlaydistricts), [historic districts](https://www.cambridgema.gov/gis/gisdatadictionary/historical/historical_historicdistricts), [conservation districts](https://www.cambridgema.gov/gis/gisdatadictionary/historical/historical_nhoodconservationdist), and [landmarks and preservation easements](https://www.cambridgema.gov/gis/gisdatadictionary/historical/historical_landmarkseasements). A mapped hit or unavailable layer puts automated replacement pricing on hold. The app also flags reported building ages at or above the 50-year screening boundary and known buildings with an unreported age. Cambridge's [demolition review](https://www.cambridgema.gov/historic/districtshistoricproperties/demolitionreview) applies to buildings over 50 years old. The city can determine significance, timing, and which approvals a specific proposal requires. Parcel frontage, current amendments, qualifying open space, surveyed setbacks, and Green Factor remain separate checks.

For a conceptual C-1 envelope above both three stories and 35 feet, the approval details link to the city's [neighbor notification instructions](https://www.cambridgema.gov/cdd/zoninganddevelopment/developmentreview/residentialneighbornotification) under §5.40 footnote 37. The actual proposal and Planning Board review path determine whether notice and a meeting are needed before a building permit. Neighbor feedback is nonbinding. This informational step does not set the replacement pricing hold.

The 95 and 106 Magazine Street report regressions use recorded assessor inputs and simplified rectangular geometry to check approval and pricing behavior. Actual footprint, surveyed setbacks, and a site-specific approval path require independent corroboration.
